# Learning

> An investor's field guide — the financial and investment concepts behind every InvestSkill framework, explained from first principles. No finance degree required. Read it straight through as a course, or jump to the lesson you need. New to the *tools*? See [Concepts](concepts.html) for the mental models and the [Glossary](glossary.html) for any single term.

**Who this is for:** anyone who wants to understand *why* an analysis says what it says — not just read the output. Every skill in InvestSkill computes something an experienced investor would look at. This guide teaches the ideas underneath, so the numbers become decisions.

## The learning path

Two parts. **Part I — Analyze a business (Lessons 1–8)** covers everything the plugin measures — from what a share is, to what a business is worth, to how to hold a portfolio without getting hurt — and puts it all together on real stocks. **Part II — Practical foundations (Lessons 9–13 and a capstone)** covers what a person meets in the first months of actually investing: accounts and orders, an ETF core, taxes, earnings season, and the psychology that protects the plan — ending with a case where the right answer is *no*.

## The one-paragraph version

A **stock** is part-ownership of a real business (Lesson 1). To judge that business you read its **financial statements** (Lesson 2), which tell you whether it's a **high-quality** operation earning more than its cost of capital (Lesson 3). Quality alone isn't enough — you also need to know what it's **worth** and to buy with a margin of safety (Lesson 4). The **market** adds timing and confirmation through price action, positioning, and the macro backdrop (Lesson 5). You hold your ideas inside a **portfolio** that's diversified, correctly sized, and protected from your own behavior (Lesson 6). And in the **capstones**, a professional runs all of it on real stocks — thesis, research plan, valuation, a written trading plan, and ongoing tracking — first on a mature compounder (Lesson 7), then on a cyclical AI-semiconductor name, AMD (Lesson 8). Part II adds the operating layer: how to **set up** accounts and orders before the first trade (Lesson 9), why an **ETF** core is where most portfolios start (Lesson 10), what **tax** does to the return and how a non-US investor is treated (Lesson 11), how **earnings season** actually works (Lesson 12), and the **psychology** that keeps the plan intact (Lesson 13) — closing with a case where the loop's correct answer is *no*.
